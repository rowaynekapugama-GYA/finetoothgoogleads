If something doesn't feel right, call us. We'll tell you how urgent it is and what to do next.

First aid

Until you get to us

Simple steps that can make a real difference before your appointment.

Knocked-out adult tooth

Hold it by the crown, not the root. Rinse gently in milk, then push it back into the socket if you can, or keep it in milk. Call us straight away.

Broken or chipped tooth

Rinse with warm water and keep any pieces. A cold compress on the cheek helps with swelling.

Toothache

Rinse with warm salty water and gently floss around the tooth. Never put aspirin on the gum.

Lost filling or crown

Keep the crown somewhere safe and avoid chewing on that side until we see you.

When to go to hospital instead. If you have swelling that spreads to your eye or neck, trouble breathing or swallowing, bleeding that won't stop or a head injury, call 000 or go to your nearest emergency department. Outside our hours, NURSE-ON-CALL is available on 1300 60 60 24.

Questions

Good to know before you visit

Can you see me today?

Call us on (03) 9723 1100 as early as you can. We'll do our best to find you the soonest appointment and talk you through what to do in the meantime.

How much does an emergency appointment cost?

It depends on what you need. We'll examine the tooth, explain your options and give you a quote before any treatment. Health fund claims are processed on the spot with HICAPS, and Afterpay is available.

What if it's after hours or a Sunday?

Leave us a message and call again when we open. If you have severe swelling, trouble breathing or swallowing, heavy bleeding or a head injury, call 000 or go to your nearest emergency department. NURSE-ON-CALL is available on 1300 60 60 24.

Should I go to hospital for a toothache?

Most toothaches are best seen by a dentist. Go to hospital if swelling spreads to your eye or neck, or you have trouble breathing or swallowing.

I'm nervous. Will it hurt?

Our first job is to get you comfortable and out of pain. We explain each step before we start and use local anaesthetic so you feel as little as possible.
